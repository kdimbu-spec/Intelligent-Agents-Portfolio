## Week 10 Activity: Deep Learning in Autonomous Vehicles

One of the most promising applications of deep learning is **autonomous vehicle (AV) technology**, which enables vehicles to navigate and make driving decisions with minimal human intervention. Companies such as Waymo are using deep learning to improve vehicle perception, prediction, and decision-making capabilities. 

Autonomous vehicles operate using data collected from cameras, radar, LiDAR, and GPS sensors. Deep learning models, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), analyse this data to identify road signs, vehicles, pedestrians, and obstacles. The system then predicts potential outcomes, plans routes, and controls vehicle actions such as steering, braking, and acceleration. 

The technology has the potential to improve road safety by reducing accidents caused by human error and increasing mobility for elderly and disabled individuals. Automated driving systems are also expected to improve traffic efficiency and support safer transportation networks. 

However, autonomous vehicles raise several ethical and societal concerns. Questions remain about accountability when accidents occur, particularly regarding whether responsibility lies with drivers, manufacturers, or software developers. Additionally, biased training data may affect decision-making performance in certain environments or situations. Privacy is another concern because autonomous vehicles continuously collect location and behavioural data, creating potential cybersecurity and surveillance risks. 
In conclusion, autonomous vehicles demonstrate the transformative potential of deep learning in solving complex real-world problems. While the technology offers significant benefits, its success will depend on responsible deployment, robust regulation, and public trust.
