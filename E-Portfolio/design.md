DESIGN BRIEFING \
This project involved developing a multi-agent research assistant to automate the retrieval, evaluation, and ranking of academic literature. The system uses a blackboard architecture, enabling specialised agents to communicate through a shared state and work collaboratively on complex research tasks.


The workflow begins with a planning agent that interprets a research question and generates a retrieval strategy. A retrieval agent collects relevant papers, while a processing agent evaluates each source for relevance and evidence quality. Structured data models implemented with Pydantic ensure reliable information exchange and maintain consistency throughout the pipeline.


A key achievement was creating a transparent decision-making process. The system not only ranks research papers but also provides relevance scores, evidence quality assessments, and explanations for its recommendations. This improves trust in the outputs and supports evidence-based decision-making.


Developing the solution strengthened my understanding of multi-agent systems, software architecture, and AI-driven research workflows. It also highlighted the importance of combining automated analysis with critical human judgement when assessing research quality.


Overall, the project successfully demonstrated how coordinated AI agents can support academic research through a scalable, modular, and transparent architecture. Future enhancements could include more sophisticated ranking methods and integration with additional scholarly data sources.
